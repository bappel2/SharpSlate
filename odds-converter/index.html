How American odds work

American odds are written around a $100 bet. A negative number such as -110 is what you risk to win $100. A positive number such as +150 is what a $100 bet wins.

So -200 marks a favorite (risk $200 to win $100) and +300 an underdog (risk $100 to win $300). Odds of +100 and -100 are the same price: an even money bet.

American odds to decimal odds and implied probability

Decimal odds are the total a $1 bet returns, stake included. For negative American odds that is 1 plus 100 divided by the odds; for positive odds, 1 plus the odds divided by 100. The implied probability is 1 divided by the decimal odds: how often the bet has to win to break even at that price.

Going back, decimal odds of 2.00 or more become positive American odds of the decimal minus 1, times 100. Below 2.00 they become negative: minus 100 divided by the decimal minus 1, so 1.80 is -125.

Why the two sides add up to more than 100 percent

Convert both sides of a market and the implied probabilities add up to more than 100 percent. A spread at -110 on both sides gives 52.4% twice, 104.8% in all. The extra 4.8 points are the sportsbook's margin, also called the vig or the hold.
