What the vig is

A sportsbook prices both sides of a market so that their implied probabilities add up to more than 100 percent. The amount past 100 is the book's margin, called the vig, the juice or the hold. It is what the book expects to keep over many bets.

Taking it out (devigging) leaves each side's fair chance as the market sees it, and the fair odds that match that chance.

How the calculator removes the vig

Each side's odds become an implied probability (1 divided by the decimal odds). The two are added, and each is divided by that total, so they add up to exactly 100 percent. The fair odds are those fair chances turned back into American odds. This is the proportional method, the most common one: it spreads the margin over both sides in proportion to their price.

What fair odds are good for

Fair odds give a price to compare against. If another book offers a side at longer odds than its fair price from a sharp market, that price pays more than the market's own estimate of the chance says it should. If every book is shorter, the margin is working against you everywhere.

SharpSlate's Prop Lab does this for every player prop on the board: it takes the margin out of each book's over and under and shows the fair odds beside the best price.

Limits of the method

The proportional method assumes the book spread its margin evenly. Books often load more of it onto the underdog, so on a lopsided market the favorite's true chance can be a little higher than this shows.

It also needs both sides. A one sided offer, such as an anytime touchdown price with no matching no, cannot be devigged this way.
