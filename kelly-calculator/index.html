The Kelly formula

The Kelly criterion sizes a bet as a share of your bankroll: the profit per $1 if it wins (the decimal odds minus 1) times your win chance, minus your chance of losing, all divided by that profit per $1. When the result is zero or below, the price pays too little for the chance you entered and Kelly stakes nothing.

Why many bettors use half or quarter Kelly

Full Kelly grows a bankroll fastest only if the win chance you enter is exactly right. Nobody's is, and staking too much does far more damage than staking too little: at twice the Kelly stake a bankroll is expected to almost stop growing.

Half or quarter Kelly gives up a little growth for much smaller swings, and leaves room for an estimate that is too high.

Where the win probability comes from

The stake is only as good as the chance you enter. A common starting point is the fair chance from a sharp market with its margin taken out (the no vig calculator does this), set against the price another book offers.

Enter a chance no higher than the book's own implied probability and Kelly stakes nothing: at that chance the price pays exactly what the bet is worth, and the book's margin usually puts the true chance below it.
