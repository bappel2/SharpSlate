Expected Value Calculator

Enter the fair chance you give a bet, the posted odds and your stake to see the bet's expected value: what it returns on average over many bets like it.

Runs on your inputs only, in your browser. Informational, not advice.

How expected value is calculated

Expected value (EV) is the average result of a bet if you could place it many times. Per $1 staked it is your win chance times the profit per $1 (the decimal odds minus 1), minus your chance of losing. Multiply by the stake for the EV in dollars.

The break even chance

A bet's EV is zero when your win chance equals the price's implied probability, 1 divided by the decimal odds. At -110 that is 52.4%; above it the EV is positive, below it negative. The odds converter shows the break even chance of any price.

Where the fair probability comes from

EV is only as good as the chance you enter. At the book's own implied probability the EV is exactly zero, and below it negative, which the book's margin makes the usual case. A common estimate is the fair chance from a sharp market with the margin taken out, which the no vig calculator works out, set against the price another book posts.

A positive EV says the price pays more than your chance is worth on average. Any single bet still wins or loses, and a few results say little about the estimate.
