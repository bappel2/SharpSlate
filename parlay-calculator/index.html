How parlay odds are calculated

A parlay pays only if every leg wins, and each winning leg multiplies the one before it. To price one, turn each leg's American odds into decimal odds, multiply the decimals together, and multiply the result by your stake. That is the total payout; take the stake back out to get the profit.

Negative American odds such as -110 become 1 plus 100 divided by 110, which is 1.909. Positive odds such as +150 become 1 plus 150 divided by 100, which is 2.50. The parlay's American odds come from the combined decimal the same way in reverse.

Pushes and voided legs

When a leg pushes (the result lands exactly on the line) or is voided, most sportsbooks drop it and pay the parlay on the legs that are left. The three leg parlay above with one push pays as a two leg parlay: +264, or $36.45 back on $10.00.

Tick Push on a leg and the calculator does the same. House rules differ, especially for a two leg parlay that loses a leg to a push, so check your book's rules.

Why parlays cost more than single bets

Every price a book posts carries its margin. A side at -110 against -110 is a coin flip once that margin is taken out, so its fair price is +100. Over three legs the fair price is +700, but the book pays +596.

That gap is the parlay's margin. On average the book keeps about 13 cents of every dollar on that three leg parlay, against about 4.5 cents on one -110 bet, because the margin compounds with each leg. Enter the other side's odds on every leg and the calculator shows this margin for your own parlay.

Fair odds for each leg

With both sides of a leg entered, the calculator takes the book's margin out the same way the Devig calculator on SharpSlate's Tools page does: each side's implied probability is scaled so the two add up to 100 percent. Multiplying the legs' fair probabilities gives the parlay's fair chance and its fair odds.

This treats the legs as independent, which is right for legs from different games. Legs from the same game often move together, so books price a same game parlay with their own model, and the multiplied price here will not match their offer. Compare the two to see how much the book moved the price.
